Station one: the rubric — a written marking scheme. Instead of one vague score out of ten, the judge grades each dimension separately: accuracy out of five, evidence out of five, tone out of five. Each level has an anchor: a plain description of what that score means, so a four means the same thing every time. Each verdict must also quote its evidence — the exact words that earned the score. Splitting one big opinion into graded parts is rubric decomposition, and it is far less noisy.
