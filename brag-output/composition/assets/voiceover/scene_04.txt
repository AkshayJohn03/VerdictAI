Some questions are comparisons: which answer is better, A or B? That is pairwise judging — models compare better than they score. But watch the order. Verdict A I asks the judge twice: A against B, then B against A. If the verdict flips when the order swaps, that is position bias: a reading habit. Flips are counted as the position-bias rate, and a flipped item scores zero point five — a tie — not a wrong answer. Two more habits are measured directly. Verbosity bias: does the score climb with length? That slope gets a statistical test. And self-preference: favoring writing from the judge's own family.
